Email Security

Learn how to protect your email account and recognize suspicious messages.

Why Email Security Matters

Email accounts often contain personal information and are connected to other important accounts. If an email account is compromised, attackers may use it to access information, reset passwords, or impersonate the account owner.

Use a Strong, Unique Password

  • Use a long and unique password for your email account.
  • Do not reuse your email password on other websites.
  • Consider using a reputable password manager.
  • Never share your password through email or messages.

Enable Two-Factor Authentication

Two-factor authentication adds another verification step after your password. This can make it harder for someone to access your account using a stolen password alone.

Enable 2FA whenever your email provider supports it, especially for accounts containing sensitive information.

Recognize Suspicious Emails

Be cautious when an unexpected email creates urgency, asks for sensitive information, or contains unfamiliar links or attachments.

  • Check the sender address carefully.
  • Be suspicious of unexpected password or payment requests.
  • Hover over links when possible before opening them.
  • Do not open unexpected attachments.
  • Verify unusual requests through another trusted channel.

Watch for Phishing

Phishing emails attempt to trick people into revealing information, clicking malicious links, opening dangerous files, or sending money. Attackers may pretend to represent banks, employers, delivery services, schools, or other trusted organizations.

Remember: a professional-looking email can still be fraudulent. Verify important requests independently instead of relying only on the message itself.

Protect Your Email Account

  • Keep your email application and device updated.
  • Review account recovery options regularly.
  • Check for unfamiliar login activity when your provider offers it.
  • Sign out of shared or public devices when finished.
  • Do not save your password on devices you do not trust.

Be Careful With Attachments

Unexpected attachments can contain malicious files. Even if an attachment appears to come from someone you know, verify unusual files before opening them because their account could have been compromised.

Quick Email Security Checklist

  1. Do I use a unique password for my email?
  2. Is two-factor authentication enabled?
  3. Did I verify the sender before responding?
  4. Have I checked suspicious links carefully?
  5. Was the attachment expected?
  6. Can I independently verify this request?

Key Reminder

Slow down when an email creates urgency or asks for sensitive information. Verify the sender, inspect links and attachments carefully, and use strong account security to reduce the risk of compromise.
