How to Secure Your Email Account

Practical steps to protect your email from phishing, unauthorized access, and account takeover.

Why Email Security Matters

Your email account can be one of the most important accounts you own. It may be used to reset passwords, receive security alerts, store personal messages, and access other online services. If someone gains unauthorized access to your email, they may also try to take over other accounts.

1. Use a Strong, Unique Password

Create a long password that is difficult to guess and do not reuse it across multiple websites. Reusing the same password means that a password exposed in one breach could potentially be used against your email account.

A password manager can help you create and store unique passwords without requiring you to memorize every password.

2. Enable Two-Factor Authentication

Two-factor authentication (2FA) adds another verification step after your password. Depending on the email provider, this may involve an authenticator app, security key, or another verification method.

Even if someone discovers your password, an additional authentication requirement can make unauthorized access more difficult.

3. Protect Your Recovery Options

Keep your recovery email address and phone number current. Recovery methods can help you regain access if you forget your password or your account becomes compromised.

Make sure your recovery information belongs to you and is itself protected with strong security settings.

4. Watch for Phishing Messages

Attackers may send emails pretending to be banks, social networks, employers, delivery companies, or other organizations. Their goal may be to trick you into revealing passwords, verification codes, or financial information.

Be cautious when a message creates urgency, requests sensitive information, or asks you to click an unexpected link. When in doubt, visit the organization's official website directly instead of using the link in the message.

5. Be Careful With Attachments

Unexpected attachments can contain malicious files. Do not open an attachment simply because it appears to come from a familiar name.

If an attachment is unexpected, verify it with the sender through a separate trusted communication method before opening it.

6. Check Account Activity

Many email providers offer security pages showing recent sign-ins, connected devices, sessions, or other account activity.

Review this information periodically. If you see activity you do not recognize, change your password and review your account's security and recovery settings.

7. Avoid Logging In on Untrusted Devices

Be cautious when signing into your email on public or shared computers. Avoid saving passwords in browsers that other people can access and always sign out when you finish.

Your personal device should also be protected with a screen lock and kept updated with security updates.

8. Never Share Verification Codes

Security codes are intended for you. A person claiming to be support, a bank employee, or another trusted organization should not need you to read a private verification code to them.

If someone asks you for a code that was sent to your phone or authenticator app, stop and verify what is happening before continuing.

9. What to Do If Your Email Account Is Compromised

  1. Change the email password immediately from a trusted device.
  2. Enable or reset two-factor authentication.
  3. Review recent account activity and remove unfamiliar sessions.
  4. Check recovery email addresses and phone numbers.
  5. Review forwarding rules and filters for suspicious changes.
  6. Change passwords for important accounts connected to the email.
  7. Contact your email provider's official support resources if necessary.

Email Security Checklist

  • Use a long, unique email password.
  • Enable two-factor authentication.
  • Keep recovery information current.
  • Be cautious with unexpected links.
  • Verify suspicious attachments before opening them.
  • Review recent account activity.
  • Never share verification codes.
  • Keep your devices and software updated.

Final Tip

Email security is not just about protecting messages. Your email account may be connected to many other digital services, so protecting it can help protect your wider online identity.
