Wi-Fi Security

Learn how to protect your devices and information on wireless networks.

Why Wi-Fi Security Matters

Wi-Fi connects phones, computers, smart devices, and other equipment to the internet. An insecure wireless network can expose users to attacks, unauthorized access, and privacy risks.

Secure Your Home Wi-Fi

  • Use WPA2 or WPA3 security when supported by your router.
  • Choose a strong and unique Wi-Fi password.
  • Change the router's default administrator password.
  • Keep your router firmware updated.
  • Disable features you do not need, such as unnecessary remote administration.

Public Wi-Fi Risks

Public networks in places such as airports, hotels, cafés, and schools can be convenient, but you should not automatically assume that every network is trustworthy.

  • Confirm the correct network name with the venue when possible.
  • Avoid entering sensitive information on suspicious networks.
  • Use HTTPS websites and keep your browser updated.
  • Turn off automatic Wi-Fi connection when you do not need it.
  • Disconnect from public Wi-Fi when you are finished using it.

Watch Out for Fake Wi-Fi Networks

Attackers can create wireless networks with names that look similar to legitimate networks. Connecting to an untrusted network can expose your activity to additional risks.

If a network name looks suspicious, asks for unusual information, or behaves differently from what you expect, do not connect until you verify it.

Protect Your Devices

  • Keep your operating system and security software updated.
  • Use a screen lock and strong device authentication.
  • Disable file sharing when using networks you do not trust.
  • Remove old or unfamiliar saved Wi-Fi networks.
  • Use mobile data instead when a wireless network cannot be trusted.

Quick Safety Checklist

  1. Is the Wi-Fi network legitimate?
  2. Is the connection protected?
  3. Am I accessing sensitive information?
  4. Are my device and browser updated?
  5. Can I use a trusted network or mobile data instead?

Key Reminder

Wi-Fi security is about both the network and the device you use. Verify unfamiliar networks, use strong security settings, keep devices updated, and avoid unnecessary exposure of sensitive data.
